About this tab — the two methods, the engine, and the desk conventions
01 · The Idea

Two honest estimates beat one confident number

Every exposure chart answers a hidden question — whose position is this? The standard answer is "nobody in particular; it's just what's on the chain." That's one honest read: pure exposure, as it sits. But the desk also carries a second, independent estimate: the dealer position, accumulated day by day from observed volume-weighted flow, signed each day by the direction of the at-the-money implied-voltage change (ΔIV). Where the two agree, conviction is cheap. Where they diverge — that's the trade-relevant surface.

A

Pure chain exposure — what's on the chain, as it sits

Every listed strike on the selected expiry, OI as-is, greeks priced at live spot and IV. No dealer attribution, no ownership claim — this is the raw exposure surface of the chain itself: where the greeks sit, regardless of who holds them or why.

B

The dealer position — built from flows

Start empty. Each trading day, take the change in open interest at every strike, weight it by that contract's vanna, and sign the whole day by the ATM ΔIV read: IV down ⇒ dealers likely sold (book long premium), IV up ⇒ bought. Days inside the ΔIV dead-band contribute exactly zero. Accumulate 150 days. Price that position with today's greeks.

quantity Σ ΔOI × vanna-wt, ΔIV-signed, 150d
sign day-level ΔIV direction (dead-band gated)
question answered what has flow actually built?

Like-for-like by construction. B is priced with the same selected-expiry chain, the same per-contract greeks, the same dollar-conversion formulas as A. The only degree of freedom is the position itself — so any difference on the chart is method, not scaling.

02 · The Engine

What actually computes

The pipeline is the modularization overhaul's payoff: one registry, one context, one archive — and a dashboard that reads the registry instead of hardcoding. The Dealer Book tab drives run_selected_modules through the same topologically-ordered execution path the CLI and orchestrator use.

StageMechanismDetail that matters
Fetch expiry_book_production.fetch_production_result One ThetaData pass: full greek chain + OI for the selected expiry, plus near/mid/far bucket books. IV-solved rows only; put-call parity mirrors vendor's unsolved legs instead of dropping real OI.
Method A dealer-frame greeks × OI × 100 GEX = γ·OI·100·S²·0.01; VEX = ±|ν|·OI·100·S·σ; CEX = χ·OI·100·S/365; DEX = δ·OI·100. Γ-flip from the execution-locus solve; gamma regime tagged AMPLIFYING/DAMPENING from net sign.
Method B · accumulate dealer_position_book.accumulate_position_book arm=div_signed: day sign from day-over-day ATM ΔIV; |ΔIV| in dead-band ⇒ day contributes 0 exactly. Per-strike weight = vanna / mean|vanna|. New/stale-vendor strikes are SOLVED, not skipped: the strike's last observed OI carries forward as the baseline, so the day's observed change prices as flow even across vendor gaps (≈8% of strike-days recovered; unobserved days cost nothing). 150-day rolling window with rollover.
Method B · price plot_flow_book_single_greek The book is a per-strike contract count (vanna-weighted, ΔIV-signed). To chart it against A, each position is valued with the identical per-contract exposure formulas and the identical chain snapshot A's charts use — same spot, IVs, DTE, multiplier. Same units, same axes; the only difference between the charts is the position itself: A = what the chain shows, B = what flow built. 221 strikes priced on the 2026-09-18 chain.

05 · Standing Conventions

What we believe while using it

ConvictionConsequence in the code
Dealers are structurally short charm Charm panels are read as liability-with-a-calendar; a single −1 flip day flips bs_charm into the dealer book. The B-engine's ΔIV signing exists precisely to track that flip.
Holding is not a default Nothing in the book assumes positions persist; B is rebuilt from flow daily, and the band layer (z, regime) judges whether today's net is unusual — not whether it's long or short.
Failure must be loud A genuine fetch/render failure is status="failed" with the real error in metrics — never a fake ok. Charting failures are best-effort and logged, never silently swallowed into blank panes.
Units are contracts, not dollars B's total_net is explicitly vanna-weighted ΔOI contracts — deliberately not comparable to A's dollar exposures. That's why B gets re-priced into A's units before any visual comparison.
Vendors leave gaps; parity fills them Unsolved deep-ITM IVs are mirrored from the opposite right via put-call parity — dropping those rows would delete real OI from the book.
